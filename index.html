Our approach

Begin with
the night

A lit crossing does not need an illuminated sky. An occupied office does not need a glowing facade. Nocturne keeps the service people need, then works backward from the night you want to restore.

01

Useful light, not spill

Exits, stairs, crossings, accessibility and required security lighting stay served. Only the light that escapes them is counted as something to remove.

02

Start with what is already there

An existing switch, timer or blind. The first actions need no new equipment and every change is manual and reversible. Staff time and permission still matter.

03

Planned is not done

A blank ledger claims no completed work. Unknown watts stay unquantified, and a receipt must match the exact project and date before it earns credit.

04

Honest about evidence

Washington results are model estimates and the demonstrations use invented data. Every claim sits on a ladder: modeled, self-reported or measured.

What you can do

Darkness you
can plan for

From one building tonight to a citywide trial, with the same accounting from a single circuit to every streetlight.

Act tonight

Turn an existing switch, timer or blind into a named-owner action, a calendar handoff and a record of what actually happened, laid over that night's real darkness and Moon.

Plan to a target

Set a sky-brightness target and find the least-cost combination of changes across buildings, signs, sports fields and streetlights, in clear skies, haze or snow.

Trial and measure

Preregister a one-building field pilot, design randomized source-isolation trials and analyze calibrated target and control readings.

Coordinate the city

Export streetlight schedules, phase a rollout, check them against each controller's declared limits and publish a shared Night Signal calendar.

They are model estimates built from public data, such as DDOT's streetlight asset layer. The restoration demonstrations use entirely invented data, and each page says so. The evidence ladder spells out what is modeled, self-reported or measured.

Planned and done are kept apart. Unknown watts stay unquantified and blinds get no electricity credit. A receipt must match the exact project and date; a service problem or a replacement floodlight withholds credit pending review. Completion is self-reported, and no circuit-level result is presented as a measured citywide reduction in skyglow.
